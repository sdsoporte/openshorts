# YouTube Shorts market research

This document summarizes the YouTube Shorts opportunity by monetization model, country value, niche, and regional content strategy. It is designed to guide channel planning and the future Trend Radar MVP.

## Executive summary

YouTube Shorts is best treated as a discovery and funnel channel, not as the primary revenue source. Shorts ad RPM is usually much lower than long-form RPM, and monetization requires high volume. The strongest strategy is to target high-value audiences with repeatable educational formats, then monetize with long-form videos, affiliates, sponsors, services, courses, templates, newsletters, or community.

Key conclusions:

- Use other channels as benchmarks for topics, hooks, pacing, thumbnails, and positioning; do not reupload or minimally transform their content.
- Prioritize countries with both advertiser value and addressable audience size.
- Build separate channels by language and audience promise instead of mixing countries/languages in one feed.
- Start with a small country basket and expand only after retention and conversion data prove a format.
- Use country-specific trends to localize the angle, not just the subtitles.

## YouTube monetization constraints

Official YouTube rules change over time; verify the current Help pages before making business decisions. Current research indicates:

- Full YouTube Partner Program access for ads generally requires 1,000 subscribers plus either:
  - 4,000 qualified public watch hours in the last 12 months, or
  - 10 million qualified public Shorts views in the last 90 days.
- Expanded YPP access can unlock earlier fan funding and Shopping features at lower thresholds, but that is not the same as Shorts ad revenue.
- Shorts ad revenue is pooled. Creators keep 45% of their allocated Creator Pool revenue.
- Reused, repetitious, minimally transformed, or copyright-risk content can be ineligible for monetization.

Practical implication: Shorts should feed a monetization system. Do not rely only on Shorts ad revenue.

## CPM vs RPM

| Metric | Meaning | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CPM | Advertiser spend per 1,000 monetized ad impressions | Useful for country/niche advertiser value, but not creator take-home. |
| RPM | Creator revenue per 1,000 total views after platform share and unmonetized views | Better for creator forecasting, but rarely public by country. |

There is no official public fixed YouTube RPM table by country. Third-party benchmarks conflict because they mix niches, dates, formats, CPM vs RPM, monetized playbacks, and channel types. Treat the ranking below as directional.

## Region strategy

### English premium channel

Target countries: United States, Canada, United Kingdom, Australia, Ireland, New Zealand.

Best niches:

- AI tools for work.
- SaaS comparisons and workflow automation.
- Personal finance and credit.
- Career, layoffs, salary, remote work.
- Real estate and cost-of-living explainers.

Best monetization:

- SaaS affiliates.
- Sponsored tools.
- Templates and courses.
- Consulting or implementation services.
- Newsletter sponsorship.

### European premium local channels

Target countries: Germany, Netherlands, Norway, Denmark, Switzerland, Sweden, France.

Best niches:

- Cost of living.
- Salaries and jobs.
- Taxes and personal finance.
- ETFs and investing basics.
- AI/productivity for small businesses.
- Immigration/relocation for professionals.

Content should be localized by language, examples, tax systems, currency, and local references. A generic translation will underperform compared with country-specific framing.

### Spanish channel

Split Spain and LATAM if the channel grows. Early on, one Spanish channel can validate formats, but country-specific series should be tagged and titled clearly.

Spain topics:

- Autónomos, taxes, jobs, salary, AI tools, productivity, ETFs.

LATAM topics:

- Freelancing in USD.
- AI for small businesses.
- WhatsApp/n8n automation.
- Personal finance, debt, saving, inflation.
- Programming with AI.
- Remote work.

Monetization in LATAM should lean toward courses, services, templates, affiliates, and community instead of ads only.

For each channel, track:

- Hook structure.
- Short duration.
- Topic category.

- Repeatable series format.

## Recommended channel plan

### Phase 1: Spanish validation channel

Audience: LATAM and Spain professionals/freelancers.

Promise: AI, automation, and practical money/work improvements.

Cadence:

- 2-3 Shorts per day for 30 days.
- 1 long-form video per week.
- 1 downloadable asset: n8n template, prompt pack, checklist, or spreadsheet.

### Phase 2: English premium channel

Audience: US/Canada/UK/Australia professionals and small businesses.

Promise: AI tools and automation that save money/time.

Cadence:

- Recreate the winning Spanish ideas in English; do not translate literally.
- Use country-specific financial/work examples.

### Phase 3: Norway/Nordic test

Audience: Norway-based professionals, immigrants, or small business owners.

Promise: practical explainers about cost of living, jobs, taxes, finance, and AI tools.

Cadence:

- 20-30 Shorts test batch.
- Measure view velocity, retention, subscribers per 1,000 views, and comments.

## Sources and evidence quality

High-confidence sources:

- YouTube Help: YPP eligibility, Shorts monetization policies, channel monetization policies.
- Google/DataReportal ad reach for YouTube audience sizing.

Directional/benchmark sources:

- Creator networks and third-party CPM/RPM datasets.
- Channel ranking sites.
- Public channel pages and creator analytics summaries.
